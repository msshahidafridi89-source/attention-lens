AttentionLens

An attention-aware learning prototype

Does your attention drift while you learn?

AttentionLens is a research prototype. You watch a short lesson, then answer a short quiz, while your webcam is used to estimate when your attention drifts. At the end you get a report (also as a PDF) showing when you lost focus, what it looked like, and how that relates to what you learned.

View the code on GitHub

How it works

  1. A 10-second sensor check (look at the screen, blink three times) makes sure your camera works.
  2. A 30-second calibration: you look at 9 dots so the app learns where you look on your screen.
  3. The lesson video plays. The app estimates gaze, blinks, eye closure, head pose and posture. Several times you are asked whether your mind wandered.
  4. If your attention seems to drop, the video pauses and offers a replay.
  5. A short quiz follows, one question at a time (you cannot go back). The camera stays on, and the time spent on each question is recorded.
  6. You get a report with a timeline, snapshots of the moments you were not focused, gaze and blink charts, and quiz results.

Your privacy

  • Camera video is analysed in your browser. It is never recorded or uploaded.
  • Snapshots are kept only in this page's memory. They are deleted when you close or refresh the tab, or press "Delete all my data". The PDF report is created on your device.
  • The server receives numbers (gaze, head pose, blink rate), your quiz answers and answer times. Quiz answers are sent to an LLM service (Groq) for grading. This app does not store them.
  • The lesson video is embedded from YouTube (privacy-enhanced mode), so YouTube may receive your IP address when the video loads.
  • This is an approximate estimate from a webcam, not a medical or psychological assessment.
